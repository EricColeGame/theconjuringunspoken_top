| Component | Entry-Level Target | Comfortable Target | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|---|

If your machine sits at the entry level, you are still in good shape. Horror games reward stable frame pacing far more than raw resolution, so a locked 60 frames per second at medium settings will feel better than an unstable ultra preset.

### Update Everything Before You Install

- **Graphics drivers** — install the latest game-ready driver from your GPU vendor, not a beta build.
- **Storefront client** — update Steam, Epic, or whichever launcher you use so the download does not stall.
- **Windows updates** — pending updates can interrupt a long download at the worst moment.
- **Storage check** — clear space for the game plus future patches, which are often large.
- **Overlay software** — temporarily disable capture and overlay tools if you hit crashes.

### Tune Your Setup for Maximum Dread

Headphones matter more than any graphics setting here. Positional audio is what tells you something moved behind you, and a good headset turns that information into tension. Play in a dark room, keep notifications muted, and consider a controller if you prefer leaning back rather than hunching over a keyboard.

## Tips for a Stronger First Playthrough

1. **Play with headphones and no distractions.** Horror design assumes you can hear the room around you.
2. **Resist the urge to sprint.** Movement noise is frequently the trigger that draws attention.
3. **Read everything.** Notes and recordings usually contain the clues that solve a section faster than trial and error.
4. **Adjust brightness honestly.** Calibrate so dark corners are dark but navigable — not so bright that the atmosphere evaporates.
5. **Save deliberately.** If manual saves are available, use them before entering a new area.
6. **Take breaks.** Tension is the product; stepping away for ten minutes resets your nerves and improves your awareness.
7. **Avoid spoiler threads until you finish.** Community puzzle solutions spread quickly after launch.
8. **Consider a second run on a higher difficulty.** It often changes enemy behavior, not just damage numbers.
