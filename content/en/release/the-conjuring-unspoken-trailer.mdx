## Official vs. Fan-Made: How the Footage Actually Differs

The Conjuring Universe has one of the most devoted fan-editing communities in horror. Talented editors cut together clips from previous films, add color grading and sound design, and produce something that genuinely looks like a studio teaser. The table below breaks down the telltale differences.

| Signal | Official Trailer | Fan-Made Trailer |
|---|---|---|
| Upload channel | Studio or verified distributor channel | Personal channel, often recently created |
| Music | Licensed or original score, credited | Reused tracks from other film trailers |
| Footage source | Newly shot material | Recycled shots from earlier franchise films |
| Description | Includes cast, crew, and release date | Vague, emoji-heavy, or "leaked" claims |
| Monetization | Standard studio distribution | Often ad-heavy or link-farm driven |
| Comment section | Mixed, moderated | Full of "this is fake" replies |

The fastest test is the footage itself. If you recognize a shot from *The Conjuring 2* or *The Nun II* with a different color grade slapped on top, you are watching a fan edit — no matter how convincing the thumbnail looks.

## How to Verify a Trailer in Under a Minute

You do not need to be a detective. Run this checklist before you believe anything you watch.

| Step | What to Check | What It Proves |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Channel name and verification badge | Studio-backed uploads come from official accounts |
| 2 | Upload date vs. announcement date | Real trailers follow announcements, not the reverse |
| 3 | Description credits | Official posts list cast, director, and rating details |
| 4 | Comment sentiment | Fan communities flag fakes fast |
| 5 | Reverse image search on the thumbnail | Exposes reused stills from older films |

- **Check the studio's own channels first.** If a trailer is real, it will exist there.
- **Be suspicious of "leaked" language.** Studios do not describe their own marketing as leaks.
- **Ignore countdown timers.** Fake uploads use urgency to farm clicks.
- **Cross-reference the rating.** An official trailer carries an MPAA rating card.

The same discipline applies to stills and posters circulating on social media. A single convincing image often spawns a dozen fake "trailer" videos within days.
