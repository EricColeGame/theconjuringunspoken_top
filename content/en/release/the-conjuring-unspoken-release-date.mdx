## Release Date Rumors, Myths, and Community Reports

The gap between "announced" and "released" is where misinformation thrives. Here are the patterns fans run into most often, based on community reports across horror fandom.

- **The placeholder date.** Streaming platforms and ticket sites sometimes auto-generate a date to fill a database field. It gets scraped, shared, and treated as news. It usually is not.
- **The "leaked" poster.** Fan-made art spreads quickly and often carries a fake date that gets repeated for months.
- **The AI trailer.** Synthetic footage of familiar characters fools casual viewers, and its "release date" card is invented.
- **The reposted old headline.** A dated article about an earlier franchise entry gets reshared without its year, creating the illusion of fresh news.
- **The "confirmed" claim with no source.** If a post cannot point to a studio statement or a trade report, treat it as a rumor.

The safest rule is simple: a release date is real when the studio says it is real, and not one moment before. Everything else is entertainment.
